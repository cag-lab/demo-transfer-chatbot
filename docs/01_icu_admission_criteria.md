# ICU Admission and Ward-to-ICU Transfer Criteria

**Policy number:** LGH-ICU-101 (synthetic)
**Owner:** Department of Critical Care Medicine
**Last reviewed:** March 2026

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines which adult inpatients should be considered for transfer from a general ward, the Emergency Department (ED), or the post-anesthesia care unit (PACU) to the Medical/Surgical Intensive Care Unit (ICU) at Lakeside General Hospital.

## 2. Scope

Applies to all adult patients (age 18 and older). Pediatric patients are covered by policy LGH-PICU-201 and are out of scope.

## 3. Who May Request an ICU Consult

Any attending physician, resident, advanced practice provider, or charge nurse may request an ICU consult. A bedside nurse who is concerned about a patient may activate the Rapid Response Team (see LGH-ICU-104) without first obtaining physician approval.

## 4. Physiologic Triggers for ICU Consultation

An ICU consult should be requested when any of the following is present and not rapidly reversible:

- Systolic blood pressure below 90 mmHg, or mean arterial pressure below 65 mmHg, after 30 mL/kg of crystalloid
- Need for any vasopressor infusion
- Respiratory rate above 30 breaths/min sustained for more than 15 minutes
- SpO2 below 90% on 6 L/min nasal cannula, or need for high-flow nasal cannula above 40 L/min or FiO2 above 0.6
- Need for non-invasive positive pressure ventilation for more than 2 hours
- Glasgow Coma Scale of 8 or below, or a drop of 2 or more points within 4 hours
- Serum lactate of 4 mmol/L or higher
- Modified Early Warning Score (MEWS) of 7 or higher

## 5. Priority Levels

| Priority | Description | Target time to ICU bed |
|---|---|---|
| Priority 1 | Unstable, needs interventions only available in ICU (e.g., intubation, vasopressors) | Within 1 hour |
| Priority 2 | Needs intensive monitoring; may need immediate intervention | Within 4 hours |
| Priority 3 | Critically ill but limited likelihood of recovery, or limits on interventions | Case-by-case discussion with ICU attending |
| Priority 4 | Not appropriate for ICU (too well or too sick to benefit) | Not admitted; document rationale |

## 6. ICU Consult Response Times

- The ICU fellow or intensivist must see a Priority 1 patient at the bedside within **15 minutes** of the consult page.
- For Priority 2 consults, the bedside evaluation must occur within **30 minutes**.
- If the ICU team cannot meet these times, the ICU attending must be notified directly.

## 7. Accepting Physician

The **ICU attending physician** has final authority to accept or decline an ICU admission. Declined consults must include a documented note with the rationale and a recommended follow-up plan, including a reassessment time no later than 4 hours.

## 8. Disputes

If the referring attending disagrees with an ICU decline, they should first speak directly with the ICU attending. Unresolved disputes are escalated to the **Administrator on Call (AOC)** and the Chief Medical Officer's designee.

## 9. Code Status

Code status must be documented before transfer whenever possible. A Do-Not-Resuscitate order does not by itself exclude ICU admission.
