# Inter-Facility Transfer of Critically Ill Patients

**Policy number:** LGH-ICU-103 (synthetic)
**Owner:** Transfer Center and Department of Critical Care Medicine
**Last reviewed:** May 2026

## 1. Purpose

Defines how critically ill adults are transferred **into** Lakeside General Hospital ICU from outside facilities, and **out of** Lakeside to another facility for services not available here.

## 2. The Transfer Center

All inbound and outbound transfers are coordinated through the **Lakeside Transfer Center**, staffed 24/7 by transfer nurses. Referring facilities call the Transfer Center line; individual physicians should not accept outside patients directly.

## 3. Inbound Transfers

1. The transfer nurse collects demographics, diagnosis, vital signs, and the reason for transfer.
2. The transfer nurse conferences the referring physician with the **ICU attending**, who decides whether to accept.
3. The House Supervisor confirms bed availability. If no ICU bed is available, the ICU attending and House Supervisor decide whether the patient can be accepted to a holding location (e.g., PACU) or must be declined.
4. Accepted patients receive a **Transfer Acceptance Number**, recorded in the transfer log.
5. The decision must be communicated to the referring facility within **30 minutes** of the initial call.

## 4. Outbound Transfers

Patients are transferred out only for a service Lakeside cannot provide (for example: ECMO, burn care, or transplant evaluation).

- The treating attending documents the reason for transfer and that the patient (or surrogate) consented.
- The patient must be stabilized within Lakeside's capabilities before departure.
- A **physician-to-physician** conversation with the accepting facility is required.

## 5. Transport Mode and Escort

| Patient condition | Transport | Escort |
|---|---|---|
| On vasopressors or mechanical ventilation | Critical care ground or air transport | Critical care transport RN and paramedic, or respiratory therapist |
| Stable, needs cardiac monitoring | Advanced Life Support ambulance | Paramedic |
| Stable, no monitoring needs | Basic Life Support ambulance | EMT |

Air transport is considered when ground transport time exceeds **60 minutes** for a Priority 1 patient.

## 6. Required Documentation Sent With the Patient

- Copy of the transfer consent and physician certification
- Most recent 24 hours of nursing notes and vital signs
- Medication administration record
- Imaging on a disc or via the regional image exchange
- Code status documentation

## 7. Regulatory Note

All transfers must comply with applicable federal and state transfer laws. Questions about legal requirements go to the Office of Risk Management.
